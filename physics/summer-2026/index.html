    • Some reports from Summer 2026

    This summer, I stayed over on campus to try and do some preliminary readings for my MS Thesis at the EPQM Group.

    My supervisor handed me a few things to read, in order:

    1. Condensed Matter Physics - The Very Short Introduction, Ross H. McKenzie (link): A very nice introductory book, goes over the various phenomena that interests condensed matter physicists, and how they approach their study.
    2. Emergent Particles and Gauge Fields in Quantum Matter, Ben J. Powell (link): A review article, goes over the moving parts of the “Standard Model of Materials” (A.K.A. The GLW Paradigm), and then moves on to a description of several quantum matter systems that fall outside of that scaffold. A fair bit more technical than Ross’ book.
    3. Condensed Matter Physics: Does Quantum Mechanics Matter?, Michael E. Fisher (link): On the ocassion of a symposium in the honor of Niels Bohr, Michael Fisher wrote this brief overview of condensed matter physics as it stood then. It was written at an interesting turning point in the history of condensed matter physics - right about the time when Quantum Hall Effects and High-Temperature Cuprate superconductors were taking center stage.
    4. There’s plenty of room in the middle: The unsung revolution of the renormalization group, Nigel Goldenfeld (link): An article that lends support to Fisher’s critical view of quantum mechanics leaking into condensed matter physics, from a more modern perspective.
    5. The Physics of Quantum 2.0: Challenges in understanding quantum matter, Siddhartha Lal and Mayank Shreshtha (link): An article written by my supervisor, which starts off by reviewing that historical stepping-stones in the study of condensed matter, and the questions/challenges that theorists have to face now.

    I wrote some reports based on my reading of these sources, which you may find below. They aren’t the best - but atleast they were written by a human :)
